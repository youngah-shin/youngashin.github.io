Young Ah Shin

Ph.D. Candidate · Privacy-Enhancing Technologies Lab, Korea University

I design cryptographic primitives and protocols that keep systems trustworthy when the devices running them are weak, mobile, or partly compromised — and I prove that they hold.

My dissertation, supervised by Prof. Ik Rae Jeong, develops practical and provably secure online/offline protocols for mobile applications: identity-based multi-proxy signatures for Internet-of-Drones deployments, and on/offline signcryption that survives memory corruption between the offline and online phases. Alongside this, I work on privacy-preserving and auditable blockchain payments, CBDC tracing, and privacy-preserving federated learning.

I expect to defend in August 2026 and am looking for postdoctoral positions in applied cryptography and privacy-enhancing technologies.

Research directions

01

Online/offline cryptographic protocols

Signatures and signcryption for mobile, multi-user, and multi-device settings, with security proofs that account for state exposure between phases.

02

Private and auditable payments

Payment channel hubs and CBDC designs that reconcile transaction privacy with fine-grained, regulation-compliant auditability.

03

Autonomous-agent transactions

Cryptographic authorization, user pre-approval, and selective disclosure for accountable payments between autonomous AI agents.
