Young Ah Shin

Ph.D. Candidate · Privacy-Enhancing Technologies Lab, Korea University

My research focuses on cryptographic approaches to enhancing trust and privacy in emergingtechnologies — particularly Internet-of-Things, federated learning, and blockchain — through the design of advanced cryptographic primitives and privacy-preserving protocols with provable security guarantees.

My dissertation, supervised by Prof. Ik Rae Jeong, develops practical and provably secure identity-based online/offline protocols for resource-constrained mobile environments: multi-proxy signatures that keep each proxy signing key independent of the long-term private key, and on/offline signcryption schemes that stay secure against memory corruption between the offline and online phases. Alongside this, I work on privacy-preserving yet auditable payment protocols (AI-agent payments, blockchain payment channel hubs, and CBDC) and on security protocols for constrained and distributed settings such as V2X, Internet-of-Drones, and federated learning.

Having defended my dissertation, I will graduate in August 2026 and am seeking postdoctoral positions in applied cryptography and privacy-enhancing technologies.

Research Interests

01

On/offline cryptographic protocols

Relocating expensive computation into an offline phase for an efficient online phase, with security proofs that hold even when precomputed state is exposed.

02

Privacy-preserving AI-agent payments under human authority

Enabling AI agents to transact autonomously while a human stays in the loop for high-stakes decisions, so that control remains with the principal.

03

Scalable and privacy-preserving payment channels

Designing multi-party payment channel hubs without a trusted third party, or guaranteeing payment unlinkability alongside accountability, with extensions to cross-chain and cross-border settings.
