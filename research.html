Overview

Research

Cryptographic approaches to trust and privacy in emerging systems — designed as primitives, analysed under standard models, and evaluated against the constraints of real devices.

Research statement

Most privacy failures in deployed systems are not failures of the underlying mathematics. They come from the gap between a clean security model and a device that runs out of battery, delegates authority to an intermediary, or leaks intermediate state between two phases of a protocol. My work targets that gap: I build cryptographic primitives whose security definitions include these operational realities, and then prove the constructions meet them.

Three threads run through my research — delegation (who may sign on whose behalf, and with which key), phase separation (what an adversary learns between precomputation and use), and selective accountability (how a system can be private by default yet auditable when policy demands it).

Doctoral research

Practical Secure Design of Online and Offline Cryptographic Protocols for Mobile Application — Korea University, School of Cybersecurity.

Part I

Identity-based multi-proxy signatures for the Internet of Drones

Establishes why a proxy signing key must be separated from the long-term private key in public-key settings, proposes a provably secure identity-based multi-proxy signature scheme on that basis, and extends it to the online/offline setting so that a resource-constrained drone performs only cheap operations in flight.

Part II

On/offline signcryption secure against memory corruption between phases

Identifies a previously overlooked threat in multi-device deployments: offline precomputed state can be read or corrupted before the online phase consumes it. Formalises the threat and gives two identity-based on/offline signcryption schemes with provable security against it.

Areas of interest

Digital signatures and provable security

  • Identity-based signatures, proxy and multi-proxy signatures, multi-signatures, adaptor signatures
  • Online/offline signatures and signcryption for mobile and multi-device systems
  • Security models and reduction-based proofs, including models that capture state exposure

Blockchain privacy and scalability

  • Payment channels, payment channel hubs, and leader election for multi-party channels
  • Anonymous payments with fine-grained auditing
  • CBDC privacy, regulation-compliant tracing, and digital-asset transaction analysis

Privacy-preserving artificial intelligence

  • Horizontal and vertical federated learning, including defences against passive label inference
  • Metadata leakage in collaborative learning, such as local training dataset size
  • Cryptographically authorised and accountable transactions between autonomous agents
